Why pruning helped — DTI algebra (verified in vendor/gems57/metric.py)

Official metric is distance-weighted Tversky with α=0.2, β=0.8, triangular kernel R=300 m (3 px): https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/ Reference loss uses same α,β: https://github.com/drivendataorg/gems-prize-reference-solution

For binary dots, let: - T = Σ_g max_x p(x)·k(d(x,g)) (coverage over truth cells, saturates at 1/truth cell) - M = Σ_x max_g k(d(x,g)) (credit per dot) - n = |P|, K = |G| - D = T + α·(n - M) + β·(K - T) ; DTI = T / D

Adding one dot with self-credit k and marginal coverage dT (≤k):

dDTI > 0  ⇔  dT > α·DTI·(dT + 1 - k)

Only when dT == k (non-redundant) does this reduce to k > α·DTI. A dot that covers multiple uncovered truth cells has dT > k? No, dT ≤ k with equality when it covers only new truth cells. Earlier prose dT ≤ k was corrected in vendor/gems57/metric.py docstring; executable arithmetic was already correct.

Efficiency mechanism: A new dot near the catalogue but far from hidden truth has low k (distance to hidden truth >R) and dT ≈ 0 → dT < α·DTI·(1 - k) → DTI decreases. Deleting such dots raises DTI even though n drops, because FP_w = n - M shrinks. H33's sequential improvements (0.2600 → 0.2708 → 0.2778) are consistent with removing low-k mass.

Can we beat 0.2778 (or the prompt's 0.3195 / 0.4413 / 0.3774)? User-reported leaders conflict and have no ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED receipt here. Leaderboard https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/leaderboard/ was not retrieved this session. We therefore treat 0.2778 as the highest byte-verified family artifact, not a current competition best.

A higher score requires: 1. Same sparsity (~37k dots) to keep n low, but better-placed dots to increase T per dot (higher k and dT against hidden truth). Random with same n needs k > 0.055 on average; H33's pruning achieved this by avoiding catalogue-flanks where hidden truth is apparently not. 2. Geological plausibility: hidden faults should be where geophysics says cover-buried structure, not where prior sparse heuristics happened to sample.

Discordance corridor is designed for (1)+(2): it biases placement toward gravity/basement-supported lineaments lacking topographic scarps, which is exactly where H33's retained dots reside (median 1.965 km from catalogue).

Caveat: HOLDOUT-DTI on catalogue-withheld folds (v1: 0.010 vs random 0.047; v2 candidate: 0.00 vs random 0.023) cannot predict hidden-label DTI. Withheld components are mapped faults; pruning that helps on hidden truth hurts on withheld mapped faults (they are near-catalogue by definition). Our holdout is a diagnostic of over-pruning, not a guarantee of leaderboard gain.

Why hidden faults are plausible targets

Great Basin extension creates high-angle normal faults; Quaternary alluvium often buries scarps while gravity (isostatic anomaly) and basement depth retain offset. GeoDAWN's 400-m magnetic/radiometric grids at 150–200 m terrain clearance resolve 100-m-scale lineaments under cover. H33's retained dots are on average nearly 2 km from mapped faults, indicating hidden truth may be not random near known faults but in covered basins—exactly where discordance is high.
