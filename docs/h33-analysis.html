Why the geometry could help

For the shared local DTI implementation, DTI = T / (T + 0.2*(S-M) + 0.8*(N-T)). Here T sums distance-weighted truth coverage, S is emitted mass, M is nearest-truth credit at prediction cells, and N is truth count. The 300m triangular kernel makes concentrated, redundant predictions potentially less efficient than well-spaced coverage. Deleting dots that obtain little hidden-truth credit can reduce false-positive cost. These are plausible explanations for the user-reported changes, not a controlled causal explanation of the organizer score. Sparse spacing alone does not guarantee improvement: dropping a useful dot can reduce recall.

This measures recovery of hidden catalogue segments, not the organizer's newly mapped faults. A large win here does not imply a leaderboard win.

Paired delta −0.01261, CI [−0.02407, −0.00180]. This proxy is not a catalogue holdout or an organizer score. Nearby SGMC/QFaults traces can be re-digitizations; removing a narrow band does not establish independence.
