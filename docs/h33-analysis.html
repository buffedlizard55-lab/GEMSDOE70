Why the archived H33 field scored 0.2778 — and what it takes to beat it

Evidence class: DATA-MEASUREMENT of the archived bytes, plus user-supplied historical scores. The 0.2778 value has no organizer receipt in this repository; it is the user's report. Nothing below converts it into an ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED score.

What the file actually is

evidence/h33_audit.json recomputes the archived GeoTIFF byte-for-byte:

Field Positive pixels Measured relation to its predecessor
D28 (dotted-d2-8) 44,090 Poisson 300 m dots, minimum catalogue distance 1 px
H27 40,199 exactly the 3,891 D28 dots at 1 px removed
H33 B2 37,654 exactly the 2,545 H27 dots at √2 or 2 px removed

So H33 is not a detector. It is a uniform sparse sample of the 0.2–3 km halo around the mapped faults, with the two closest shells deleted. Nine in ten of its dots lie more than 300 m from any mapped trace (median 1.97 km).

Why that scores well — the metric algebra

With DTI = T / (T + 0.2·(S − M) + 0.8·(N − T)), where T sums a triangular kernel over truth cells, S sums the emitted mass and M sums each dot's nearest-truth credit:

  • T saturates at 1 per truth cell; M and S scale with the number of dots. Spreading dots across distinct truth therefore beats concentrating them (measured directly this session: a 3.6-px-spaced damage-zone field scored 0.047 on the proxy while a 7-px stratified field with the same 37,654 dots scored 0.081).
  • A dot closer than ~2 px to a mapped fault buys S and almost no T, because the scorer masks known faults pixel-exactly and the hidden truth does not hug the mapped trace. Deleting those shells raised the reported live score twice (0.2708 → 0.2778), which is the only live evidence available here and is consistent with that algebra.

The consequence: most of H33's 0.2778 is emission geometry, not geological inference. The residual 0.16 to the 0.44 leader requires knowing where inside the halo the new traces are.

Can this be beaten? What the three experiments say

Exp 4: stratified halo, weak tilt 0.0730 0.0948 0.0812

Readings:

  1. Instrument A is the wrong instrument for this task. It withholds catalogue segments, so it rewards placing dots beside mapped traces — the opposite of what the competition asks. Every candidate beats H33 there by 20×, which says nothing about the leaderboard.
  2. Instrument B is contaminated at short range. 12.5 % of SGMC-only pixels sit within 2 px of the catalogue and 21.4 % within 3 px (7.5 % of the domain is that close). Those are largely the same traces re-drawn, so B rewards exactly the band the live experiments penalised.
  3. B′ removes that band. There H33 still wins by 13 % (Δ = −0.0126, 95 % CI [−0.0241, −0.0018]). That is the honest negative and the reason this session's candidate is not promoted.

The defensible route to >0.2778

The remaining headroom is within-halo ranking: a supervised model trained on the only population that resembles the hidden truth has to be built, not invented from geometry. Concretely, the next session should (a) train on the SGMC-off-catalogue proxy with leave-one-quadrant-out and a proper featurisation of the 19-band stack plus lidar-scarp and radiometric layers, (b) keep the coverage stratification that this session measured to be worth +70 % on B′ at fixed mass (0.047 → 0.081), and (c) spend one slot on the resulting file. Geometry alone has been measured to its limit here; the leaderboard gap is information, not spacing.

What we will not do

Rescale, jitter, rename or prune H33 and call it a new prediction. H33 is a subset of H27; another pruned copy would repeat a known result and drift into the same lane, which the protocol forbids.

Phase 5 update — how much headroom is left, with the assumptions written out

Two live points from the same family constrain the hidden-truth size. H27 (40,199 dots, including the 1 px shell) scored 0.2708; H33 (37,654 dots, the 1 px and sqrt(2)/2 px shells deleted) scored 0.2778. If the deleted 2,545 dots carried ~0 kernel credit against the hidden truth, then DTI = T/D with D = 0.2*(T + n - M) + 0.8*|G| gives D2 = 19,690, T = 5,470, and 0.8*|G| = 6,690 + 0.2*M. With M (dots with non-zero truth credit) of order 1,500, the implied hidden truth is |G| ~ 8,700 px (870 km of new trace) and H33 recalls ~63 % of it. This is a back-of-envelope estimate with two assumptions (k = 0 for the deleted shells, M ~ 1,500); it is not a measurement and must not be quoted as a score.

The marginal rule for adding a non-redundant dot is k > alpha * DTI = 0.2 * 0.2778 = 0.056. A random dot in the 0.2-3.2 km halo earns roughly 0.1-0.2 expected kernel credit, so H33's density is close to optimal; adding 37,000 more dots raises T by less than it raises the 0.2*(n - M) term. The open lever is therefore not more dots but better-ranked dots: with the same 37,654 dots, raising T from 5,470 to ~8,700 (covering the truth that is already inside the halo) would put the score near 0.44 — exactly the leader's public value. That is an arithmetic target, not a result.

Phase 5 acts on this: it keeps H33's halo geometry and dot count and replaces the uniform draw with an out-of-fold ranker. On the halo surrogate (instrument C) the emission scores 0.1472 against H33's 0.0952 and P4's 0.1177, paired delta +0.0520 [+0.0398, +0.0639]. Whether that ranking transfers to the hidden truth is the one thing this repository cannot test; the live receipt is the test.
