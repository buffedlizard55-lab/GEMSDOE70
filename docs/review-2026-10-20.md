## What can be established from sources

### Competition facts

The official [problem description](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/page/967/) says the task is to predict geological faults indicative of geothermal resources. It says the known public fault data are incomplete and that expert-labelled faults missing from the existing public database comprise the test set. Thus a catalogue-only reconstruction can support debugging or proxy experiments, but cannot by itself demonstrate discovery of genuinely missing faults.

The official page specifies distance-weighted Tversky scoring with a 300 m triangular kernel and alpha 0.2 / beta 0.8. It specifies a single-layer float32 GeoTIFF in EPSG:32611 at 100 m, matching training-data bounds/shape, prediction values between 0 and 1, and null/NaN outside the bounds. These statements are directly reviewable on the linked page. The competition's current sample/template must still be used to verify exact geotransform and outside-footprint behavior; none is present here.

The current [official public leaderboard](https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/leaderboard/) was fetched on 2026-10-20. **ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED public leaderboard: 0.4413** (top entry at retrieval time). This is the leaderboard value displayed by DrivenData, not a score for any local candidate. It supersedes lower “current high” values in the prompt as a current-public-board snapshot; leaderboard standings can change after retrieval.

### GEMSDOE32 comparison and score ambiguity

The user-provided [GEMSDOE32 page](https://buffedlizard55-lab.github.io/GEMSDOE32/docs/index.html) was read in full. It describes its H33-2-B2 file as a candidate, gives owner-reported proxy/model analyses, and explicitly states that no organizer score exists for that artifact. Its displayed holdout/projection tables are not competition receipts. Therefore the numerical score attributed to that file in the prompt cannot be independently confirmed from that page as an organizer score, and cannot be used here as a verified benchmark.

The page's scientific explanation for why sparse, spatially distributed predictions might do well is plausible under the official metric: the metric rewards proximity to truth, penalizes predicted mass, and uses a distance tolerance; concentrating limited prediction mass near likely fault traces can improve credit per predicted pixel. But that is a mechanism-level explanation, **not a verified causal explanation of the cited score**. The page itself reports that its local holdout uses visible catalogue truth and cannot reward a truly new fault, and that some of its live-truth numbers come from owner-derived model assumptions. Those analyses should not be conflated with the official hidden-set score.

### Source-supported scientific context

The [USGS GeoDAWN release](https://www.usgs.gov/data/geodawn-airborne-magnetic-and-radiometric-surveys-northwestern-great-basin-nevada-and) describes airborne magnetic and radiometric surveys intended to support geologic/geophysical mapping and geothermal/critical-mineral studies, and notes related lidar topography. The competition page lists supplied features including conductivity, strain, gravity, magnetic, and earthquake-density layers. The [INGENIOUS project page](https://gbcge.org/current-projects/ingenious/) describes its geothermal exploration aim and links to public data releases on heat flow, regional geophysical maps, electrical conductance, and fault slip/dilation tendency. These sources establish that relevant data products exist; they do **not** establish that those products are present in this checkout, aligned to the competition grid, licensed for this submission, or predictive in spatial holdout.
