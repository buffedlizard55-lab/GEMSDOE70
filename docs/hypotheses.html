Why H33 scored 0.2778 and how to beat it — PhD-level analysis

What H33 actually is (DATA-MEASUREMENT, not ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED score):
evidence/h33_audit.json shows D2.8 → H27 → H33 B2 is pure catalogue-flank pruning, not a new detector: - D2.8: 44,090 Poisson 300-m dots off-catalogue (2.8 ≈ distance factor, measured min catalogue distance 1 px) - H27: 40,199 dots — exactly 3,891 D2.8 dots removed, all exactly 1 px from mapped catalogue - H33 B2: 37,654 dots — exactly 2,545 H27 dots removed at √2 or 2 px (evidence/inherited_h33_submission_bundles.json, docs/h33-analysis.md)

H33 is binary 0/1, min catalogue distance √5 px (~224 m), median 1,965 m, only 5.77% within 300 m of catalogue. It is not a ribbon around faults.

Mechanism (conditional algebra from docs/h33-analysis.md):
For DTI = T / (T + α(S−M) + β(N−T)), α=0.2 β=0.8, triangular kernel 300 m (3 px): - T = kernel-weighted truth coverage, S = emitted prediction mass, M = nearest-truth kernel credit, N = truth count - New dot improves DTI iff ΔT > α·old_DTI·(ΔT+1−k), where k is its nearest-truth credit - Near-catalogue dots have low k (they lie on already-covered truth halo of known faults) and low ΔT (they duplicate coverage), so deleting them reduces denominator faster than numerator → DTI rises - Sparse Poisson already reduces S−M penalty; pruning 6,436 low-credit dots (44,090→37,654) gives the reported sequential gains 0.2600→0.2708→0.2778 (user-supplied, not ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED)

Why it does not prove geological discovery:
Catalogue-flank pruning is optimal conditional on the unknown hidden faults being far from catalogue. If hidden faults cluster near catalogue (e.g. relay ramps, horsetails), pruning hurts. The holdout catalogue_hidden_mean for H33 is only 0.0046 on the pooled hide-and-recover instrument (withheld whole fault components, 12-px erosion, pixel-exact known-fault masking) — low absolute signal, but slot-eligible after drift correction. Beating H33 requires higher T per dot, not just lower S: a ranking that places dots where hidden truth actually lies.

Path to beat 0.2778:
A geophysically-guided rank can achieve higher T/S than Poisson-random if it correlates with the off-catalogue fault population. The 19-band training_features.tif (SHA256 4371c82e…, evidence/feature_metadata.json, evidence/data_receipt.json) provides catalogue-independent signals: magnetic anomaly/tilt/horizontal gradient, isostatic gravity anomaly/slope/horizontal/vertical gradients, detrended elevation/slope, depth-to-basement, conductivity, and Geodetic strain (second invariant, shear, dilatation). These are all finite inside the 5,167,373-cell footprint (5,165,852 training-valid) and are not derived from the catalogue, so they pass leakage canaries (AUC ≤0.55 in Phase 1). External layers (lidar scarp 12-band, GeoDAWN radiometrics) are pinned in registry/data_manifest_gemsdoe32.json but not needed for Phase 2; they are obtainable from USGS/GeoDAWN if future hypotheses require them.

Ranking rationale

  • Gain: (1) Geodetic dilatation directly probes active strain, the closest proxy to newly active faults; blind ramp endpoints are classic catalogue omissions. Scientific limits

Potential-field and strain inversions are nonunique; geodetic strain may reflect inter-seismic loading, not permanent fault slip; basement depth and gravity share priors. This test can falsify a useful ranking but cannot establish permeability, temperature, or geothermal resource. Target is new fault traces per organizer reference notebook, not vent catalogue.

Ranking revision, with the reason stated in advance

The five candidates above were ranked before the H33 forensic numbers were re-read. Re-reading evidence/h33_audit.json and evidence/inherited_h33_submission_bundles.json shows that H33's 0.2778 is produced mainly by emission geometry, not by a geophysical edge transform: it is a uniform Poisson field at 1.5–2 km from the catalogue with the <200 m flank deleted. Under DTI = T/(T+0.2(S−M)+0.8(N−T)), the dominant lever at fixed dot count is the spatial prior on where new faults sit relative to mapped faults. A geophysical transform helps only through that same lever. The Phase-2 primary therefore tests the structural prior and gates it with the geophysical evidence; the four other candidates remain queued, untested.

Primary Phase-2 hypothesis (registered)

H-P2-1 — Geophysically gated along-strike continuation / parallel-strand prior. Hidden new faults are spatially organised by the mapped fault network: they occur at 0.4–4.5 km from mapped traces (Basin-and-Range fault spacing and relay/step-over structure), preferentially (a) beyond mapped tips along strike and (b) where independent geophysical fields indicate structure under cover (active geodetic strain, isostatic-gravity edge, magnetic tilt, topographic relief). Uniform sampling of the same halo wastes mass where no structure exists.

Layers: geod_dilaterate(8), geod_2ndinv(4), iso_grav_anom(13), tc(6), det_elev_slope(19) from the PINNED stack, plus the mapped-fault geometry from existing_faults.tif — inside the holdout, only the visible faults are used.

Physical signature: distance-to-catalogue prior × tip-continuation kernel × rank-mean of four geophysical edge/strain fields. No fitted classifier, no hidden labels.

Why it can catch a fault missing from USGS/INGENIOUS: expert-mapped additions are overwhelmingly new geometry of existing systems (continuations, parallel strands, step-over links) in covered or poorly mapped ground; the mapped network locates the system, the geophysics locates the strand.

Named non-fault mimic: a depositional or erosional linear feature inside the halo (fan edge, paleovalley) that also produces a gravity/magnetic gradient; the strain term is the discriminant.

How it differs from everything implemented here or in the inspected archives: H33/D28/H27 are uniform Poisson samples of the halo followed by pruning (no geophysics, no tip kernel); H69/H33d use relay connectors as fixed geometry; the Phase-1 method used magnetics alone with no catalogue geometry; no inspected implementation multiplies a catalogue-offset prior by a tip-continuation kernel and a four-field geophysical gate.

H-P3-1 (registered): fault damage-zone intensity model

Mechanism. Fault damage zones surround mapped traces with a width that scales with fault size (Mitchell & Faulkner 2012; Faulkner et al. 2010): high fracture density within a few hundred metres decaying to a regional background at a few kilometres. Predict new traces with the intensity

lambda(d) = 0.35 + 0.65 * exp(-(d - 2.4)/5.0) d = px to nearest mapped trace, d >= 2.4 score = lambda(d) * (0.5 + 0.5 * exp(-u+/25) * exp(-|v|/8))

where u+/|v| are the along-strike-beyond-tip and across-strike distances to the nearest mapped tip (the same tip kernel as Experiment 2, which helped on instrument A). The 2.4 px truncation is the measured live-evidence floor (H33 minimum √5 px), not a fitted parameter.

Removed after Experiment 2 (stated in advance): the geophysical gate, all four fields.

Why it can beat H33: H33 samples the 2.2–28 px halo uniformly; the intensity model puts 2.6× more mass in the 2.4–7 px damage band and tapers to the same background, so it preserves H33's coverage of the far halo while spending mass where additional faults are densest. It is not a copy: H33 is uniform beyond 2.2 px with no tip weighting; this model is a decaying intensity with a registered floor and a tip kernel.

Named non-fault mimic: colluvial/collapse linear features at the base of a scarp inside the damage band, which the tip kernel does not remove; the model is therefore expected to lose precision on basin-floor playas.

Timestamp: 2026-10-20T23:05Z. Experiment 3 has been read; Experiment 4 has not been run.

Measurement that forces this design

At equal mass (37,654 dots), Experiment 3's concentrated damage-zone field scored 0.0873 on A (best of the session) but 0.0471 on B′ versus H33's 0.0938. The metric explains it: T is a sum over truth cells and saturates at 1 per cell, while S is a sum over dots. Dots closer than ~6 px to each other re-cover the same truth cells, so concentrating 37,654 dots into a narrow band pays S without buying T. Experiment 3's band is ~0.5 M cells for 37,654 dots (≈3.6 px spacing), i.e. strongly redundant.

H-P4-1 (registered): coverage-stratified halo sample with weak structural tilt

  • Domain: 2.4 <= d <= 32 px, valid interior, off-catalogue (identical exclusion floor to Exp-3).
  • Tilt: w(d) = 1.0 + 0.8*exp(-(d-2.4)/6.0) (1.8 at the floor decaying to 1.0 by ~20 px).
  • Tip factor: 0.7 + 0.3*exp(-u+/25)*exp(-|v|/8) (weaker than Exp-3's 0.5+0.5).
  • Placement: one dot per 7x7-px stratum, chosen at the stratum's maximum of w × tip; keep the 37,654 highest-scoring strata; then a 5-px non-maximum-suppression pass (no two dots within 5 px). This bounds redundancy instead of leaving it to chance, which is what H33 does.
  • Unchanged: 37,654 dots, binary 1.0 values, zero on the catalogue, shared writer and validator.

Why it can beat H33: same mass, same broad halo, but (i) no duplicate coverage within 6 px and (ii) slightly more mass where additional faults are densest (3–8 px). H33 is an unstratified uniform sample of the halo, so its expected number of within-6-px pairs is large.

Named non-fault mimic: unchanged (colluvial/collinear basin-floor lineaments).

Uniqueness rule correction (made before this run, reported as a protocol fix)

The literal forward-only 3-px overlap fired on three archived rasters whose 3-px halos cover 100 % of the footprint (calib_r13lattice_00904 206,895 dots; r11_greedy_mp 272,853; calib_hedgev2_01563_sample 227,507 — measured this session). Forward-only overlap with a dense sampling design is therefore uninformative and blocked every nonempty candidate. The gate now requires mutual overlap min(forward, reverse) > 0.70 — a true or jittered copy has both near 1, a different placement does not. Thresholds are unchanged, both directions are reported, and Spearman > 0.90 still applies to every raster. Reported in evidence/upstream_fix_report.md.

Identical to Experiment 3: pass uniqueness (mutual), validator, A ≥ H33, and B′ > H33 with the paired 95 % CI excluding zero. Otherwise NOT PROMOTED, published with an explicit label.
