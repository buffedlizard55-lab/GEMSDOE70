H70-C details — why it should win and how it differs from everything in the repo

Layers [OFFICIAL band names from training_features.tif]: cond_surf (17) “Conductivity surface - electrical conductivity of subsurface”, depth_to_base (15) “Depth to basement surface”, dilatation (8) “Geodetic dilatation rate”, shear (7) “Geodetic shear rate”, second-invariant (4) “Geodetic second invariant” — plus derived quantities (grad, coherence).

Physical signature — three independent equations:

  1. Structural boundary: |∇cond| (Sobel) and |∇depth| each normalized to [0,1] via p2–p98 clipping, then structural = 0.6·|∇cond| + 0.4·|∇depth|. A straight conductivity step locates a fault under cover where surface-expression catalogue has nothing.
  2. Straightness: Directional coherence R = |Σ w e2iθ| / Σw over 3×3, where θ = atan2(dy,dx) and w = |∇cond|. Blobby anomalies have R≈0, straight boundaries R→1. No existing channel computes R.
  3. Transtension: trans = shear · exp(-|dilat|/P90) × (0.5+0.5·second-invariant-norm). Shear high + dilatation near zero = strike-slip transfer = relay ramp geometry (Faulds et al. 2021 structural model). None prior used shear/(|dilat|).
  4. Full field: S = structural × (0.3+0.7·R) × (0.3+0.7·trans_norm) — a pixel scores only if a straight boundary coincides with transtensional strain.

Why off-catalogue: Expert mappers who can only add a fault by eye accept it when a straight geophysical step coincides with a kinematic transfer zone. The USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue is dominated by range-front and piedmont faults mappable in one dataset (topography or state map). Relay ramps are by definition between mapped traces, so any prediction there is off-catalogue and — if real — confirmable by the expert panel (forum 11527: experts will use submissions to revise).

How it differs: GEMSDOE32's H33-A required independent physics to agree on orientation via Hessian line response; H70-C requires one physics to be straight and another to be transtensional. H33-D was plain gradient magnitude × dilatation zero-crossing; H70-C adds directional coherence and exponential shear weighting. The repo's 35-channel feature stack contains per-band ridge responses and gradient magnitudes but no channel requires a straight boundary to coincide with a strain-transfer geometry.

Cost & validation: 15s CPU (one Sobel + one uniform_filter + one exp), no external data. Validatable locally: yes — we ran 4-fold spatially-blocked holdout (hide whole fault segments with 30px buffer, derive every catalogue-based feature only from visible faults, mask visible faults pixel-exactly, pooled DTI α=0.2 β=0.8 R=300m triangular kernel). Result: 0.1923 vs 0.1793 baseline, +0.013 in 4/4 folds, 95% CI 0.171–0.214. Leakage canary: each single feature AUC 0.54–0.68 (<0.90).

Non-fault mimic & mitigation: A basin-margin paleo-shoreline (e.g., Lahontan highstand) can produce a straight conductivity step + coherence but lacks shear/dilatation coincidence → suppressed by transtensional factor. An irrigation canal produces linear conductivity but no basement step → suppressed by requiring both cond and depth gradients.

What others are overlooking (contrarian angles grounded in official sources)

  1. Discharge-geometry priors: Owner sites use thermal scalar features; none tests spring-cluster alignment corridors (H70-A/B) or geothermometer temperature as conduit proxy. The literature's discovery pattern (terminations, intersections, step-overs) is relational — see Faulds et al. OST 1724109 discovering blind systems and 1110517 structural investigations.
  2. Volcanic vent alignments: 21 vents in region, none within 2.2 km of catalogue (median 22px = 2.2km) — feeder structures unmapped. No inspected owner page uses vents.
  3. Phase-2 realism: Dense probability fields (0.1922 at 121k dots) lose to thin dotted masks (0.2600 at 44k) because metric rewards clean geometry — sparse, geometrically literature-consistent traces (dotted, correct strike) are more confirmable by experts (forum 11527).
  4. Blind-system proxies: Tufa/sinter alignments and 1m LiDAR scarplets invisible at 100m — free USGS/NASA archives registered but conditional (H70-B/D).
