⚠️ The portal error Predicted values must be in range [0, 1] — fixed

DrivenData rejects files whose values fall outside [0,1]. Two mechanisms produce this with the same message:

  1. Mechanism 1 — float32 sentinel -3.4028234663852886e+38. training_features.tif uses this as nodata: 7,113,308 cells, of which 3,061 lie inside the submission footprint. Writing those through unchanged puts them outside [0,1].
  2. Mechanism 2 — NaN outside footprint. A raster with nodata=NaN and NaN cells outside footprint is allowed per spec (“data outside bounds is null or nan”) but is one validator change away from the same rejection. Every primary here is all-finite, nodata=None, zeros outside.

What this is

GEMSDOE70 is an auditable system for the DOE GEMS Prize (DrivenData #306, GeoDAWN/NW Nevada): find geothermal-indicative faults not in the USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue, and ship them as a legal GeoTIFF. It has three parts: an exact transcription of the competition metric (DTI α=0.2 β=0.8 300m triangular kernel); a preregistered spatially-blocked holdout that scores emission rules under that metric; and a slot gate that decides when a weekly submission is worth spending.

The scored population is not the training labels (60,988 USGS fault pixels in this download) but the expert-mapped new faults — see competition structure and rules PDF (SHA 50d854b1e0239fe6…).

  • Metric verified vs worked example (TPw=3.00 FPw=1.89 FNw=2.00 → TI=0.60) — tests pass pytest tests -q
  • Credit bar k > 0.2·DTI ≈ 0.052 — every emitted pixel must clear it
  • Byte identity (sha256) of every raster shipped here
  • Official source links on sources page

Not claimed: No score here is organizer-verified; numbers are labeled HOLDOUT-DTI (evaluator v2.1, 4-fold, 95% CI) or ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED (copied from submission-page receipt). A projection is never written as a score.

The brief's question, answered (PhD-level)

Why did the dotted H19-5 / h33-2-b2 family score highest?

Because the metric is a budget: DTI = TPw / (0.8·G + 0.2·(TPw+FPw)). Thinning a thick surface while keeping its geometry removes mass that was already covered (redundant TPw≈0) — it raises credit per emitted pixel and moves the file to the break-even point where marginal credit = 0.2·DTI.

  • 121,131 dots at 100m min spacing → 0.1922 (dense, redundant)
  • 60,069 at 200m → 0.2477
  • 44,090 at 282.8m (median NN exactly 300m) → 0.2600 — optimum sits at the kernel radius R where two dots stop competing for the same truth pixel
  • h33-2-b2: 37,654 at B=2 pruning → 0.2778 — pure removal of waste on catalogue (masked from scoring per forum 11516: known faults masked from evaluation). Deleting 2,545 dots with k=0 cannot hurt, and at this DTI the break-even is 0.0548 measured vs 0.0520 derived.

Can we beat 0.2778 / 0.3195?

Need ≈+23% mean credit per pixel at constant mass (0.3195/0.26). No public catalogue can supply that — newest public compilation is 59,065 px, all but one within 300m of the given catalogue. Only a better detector field that places top-ranked pixels closer to hidden faults can. GEMSDOE70's path is H70-C relay-ramp: transtensional relay ramps between en-echelon segments are blind under alluvium (catalogue maps segments, not ramps); they are precisely the hidden-fault mechanism the 300m kernel rewards. Holdout: +0.013 over baseline at matched 38.5k mass, 4/4 folds.

Holdout: does the emission rule beat the incumbent?

Four spatially blocked folds; detector never sees the block it is scored on; every rival geometry re-emitted at same pixel count. Preregistration: GEMSDOE70-PREREG-1. Leakage canary: each feature alone AUC 0.54–0.68 (threshold 0.90) → no leakage.
