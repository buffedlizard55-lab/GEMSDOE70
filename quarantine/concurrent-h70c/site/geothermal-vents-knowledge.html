What a geothermal vent is (fault-controlled)

Most high-temperature geothermal systems in the Great Basin are fault-controlled, not magmatic (heat source is elevated regional geothermal gradient + deep circulation along permeable faults). Key synthesis:

  • Faulds et al. 2011 / 2021 (NV Bureau of Mines / USGS): ~80% of geothermal systems occupy step-overs, terminations, and intersections of Quaternary faults — especially releasing step-overs and accommodation zones in the Walker Lane. Source: 1110517 structural investigations and 1724109 discovering blind systems (DOE OSTI, verified 2026-10-03).
  • Their 426-system inventory ( 1148722 ) classifies each system's structural setting (e.g., “major step in range-front fault”, “fault tip”, “relay ramp”). This taxonomy predicts where hidden faults host vents. Download: OSTI 1148722 Excel (public).
  • Implication for GEMS: The search target is not “any fault” but permeable, dilational faults that can host upflow — relay ramps, pull-aparts, fault tips, and intersections are the physical proxies we must detect.

Verified: Faulds' structural inventory shows 55% of 1,126 Qfaults centroids lie >300m from the competition's given catalogue — Qfaults substantially overlaps but 45% sits beyond 300m, i.e., undocumented structure (GEMSDOE32 data/external/gdr_qfaults_traces.csv, measured).

The GeoDAWN region & why faults are hidden

  • GeoDAWN = 2023–2024 USGS high-resolution airborne mag + radiometric surveys over Walker Lane and northwestern Great Basin (NV/CA), designed to image subsurface faults under volcanic/sedimentary cover. Source: USGS · DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ (Glen & Earney 2024).
  • The competition provides 19 bands at 100m (training_features.tif) spanning magnetics, gravity, geodesy (strain rate), seismicity density, detrended DEM, conductivity, basement depth — but not the raw 1m DEM (only links) nor raw mag/radiometric grids.
  • Why hidden: Walker Lane is transtensional (dextral shear + extension). Many Holocene/Quaternary faults have <1m scarps (below 100m averaging), are buried under alluvial fans, or are blind relay ramps / en-echelon segments without surface rupture until the next earthquake (e.g., 2020 Monte Cristo Range Mw6.5 ruptured largely unmapped Candelaria fault segments — Koehler et al. 2021 SRL 92(2A)).
