What is actually being predicted?

The target is a surface fault-location raster, not a catalogue of geothermal vents and not a map of proven commercially viable reservoirs. The organizer's problem description distinguishes pre-existing public faults, expert-labeled new faults, public/private test subsets, and a later expanded label set. A thermal anomaly may corroborate a structural interpretation; it does not independently establish a fault or permeability at depth. Conversely, an absence of a thermal surface expression is not evidence of no buried fault.

The INGENIOUS project explicitly targets hidden geothermal systems. Its GDR compilation includes spring/well chemistry, paleogeothermal deposits, geophysics, strain, and fault data. Those are opportunities for later corroboration, not permission to substitute temperatures for the competition labels.

Why the H33-2-B2 artifact is scientifically interesting

Measured, not inferred: we reopened the prior TIFF for educational inspection, verified its SHA-256, and counted its support. See reference-artifact.json. The file has 37,654 positive pixels; none are within two Euclidean grid pixels of the bridged existing-fault catalogue. The inspected artifact was not copied to this repository's submissions or download directory.

Code evidence: the pinned H33 validation source constructs H33-2-B2 by deleting near-catalogue dots from an already constructed base. The flank-sweep implementation is the relevant method, not the generic H33 hypothesis-union builder. Local source snapshots are in evidence/sources/.

Score provenance: the prompt attributes 0.2778 (USER-REPORTED; NOT ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED) to that artifact. No submission-page receipt tying that score to its exact bytes is available. The reference site's own landing page says no organizer score exists for its files, while describing model-based projections. Those historical projections are not scores and are not used as a gate here. We cannot establish from a public team leaderboard alone that this exact TIFF produced the reported result.

Plausible explanation, not proven causation: sparse predictions can preserve most weighted truth coverage while removing low-value mass. Write:

  • T = sum over truth pixels of maximum nearby probability × triangular kernel;
  • S = sum of prediction probabilities;
  • M = sum over prediction pixels of probability × nearest-truth kernel;
  • K = number of truth pixels.

The official formula becomes DTI = T / [0.2(T + S − M) + 0.8K + epsilon]. This identity keeps the asymmetric truth-side and prediction-side credits separate. Deleting a redundant prediction can reduce the false-positive term without reducing T. Deleting a useful prediction can instead harm recall. A wider buffer is therefore not automatically better. Hidden new faults can also be near mapped faults; a blanket halo exclusion can suppress real splays. A controlled same-evaluator comparison and an organizer receipt are required before attributing a reported improvement to pruning.

The break-even statement often repeated in prior sites (credit > alpha × current DTI) has restrictive assumptions. In general an added binary dot has truth-side incremental credit delta_T and self-credit k_self. It helps iff delta_T > alpha × DTI × (delta_T + 1 − k_self) (ignoring the unchanged epsilon). One dot can cover multiple truth pixels. Do not assume delta_T <= k_self or that the truth-side and prediction-side credits are interchangeable.

Can we exceed the reported reference result? Possibly, but not demonstrably from the current checkout. There is no comparable current-best holdout, new holdout measurement, or candidate model. We do not forecast a score or promise to beat the leaderboard.
